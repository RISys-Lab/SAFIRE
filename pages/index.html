SAFIRE : Safety-Critical Benchmark for Fine-grained Fire and Smoke Understanding in Multimodal LLMs

Pengfei Li Naufal Suryanto Sicheng Zhang Mohammad Alsharid Muzammal Naseer
Khalifa University
Paper Code Dataset

Abstract

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) show strong progress on vision-language tasks, yet their reliability in safety-critical settings remains underexplored. Fire-smoke understanding is central to public safety and disaster response, but existing benchmarks lack diverse real-world scenarios and context-aware evaluation. We introduce SAFIRE, the first large-scale benchmark for fire-smoke understanding in MLLMs, comprising 105K images from 20 scenarios and 224K multiple-choice VQA (MCVQA) pairs across 10 evaluation dimensions, from basic perception to higher-order reasoning. A multi-stage filtering pipeline with model-guided majority voting ensures annotation quality. Evaluating seven open-source MLLMs (7B–72B) yields 57.5% average accuracy, exposing major gaps in safety-critical reasoning. We further provide a domain-specific image-caption set and show that adapting vision encoders with only 3% of our supervised data boosts fire-scene classification by 12.7%, underscoring the inadequacy of generic features and the potential of lightweight adaptation.

Key Insight: Most models achieve mid-to-high performance on structurally straightforward tasks like Scene Classification and Spatial Correlation, but struggle significantly on causal, human-centric, or ambiguous reasoning tasks (e.g., Emotional Response, Fire Intention). Furthermore, adapting vision encoders with just 3% supervised data yields a 12.7% boost, indicating high potential for lightweight domain adaptation.
